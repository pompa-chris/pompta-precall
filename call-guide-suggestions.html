Live Call Guide — Script Suggestions

Steps 1–5 analysis. Side-by-side comparisons with rationale. Meaning is preserved — language is tightened for easier live delivery.

Step 1 Opening — “Dr. Pompa’s Office” Primary script: no change

The opening script is tight. 3-beat pattern, micro-commitment with “Can you hear me okay?” — no changes needed there. One small improvement to the follow-up:

Current Follow-Up
After they respond
“Great! I’m just calling to make sure you’re all set for your appointment with {HAName} on {Day, Date}.”
Suggested Follow-Up
After they respond
“Great! I’m just calling to make sure you’re all set for your appointment with {HAName} on {Day} at {Time}.”
Why
Adding the time here plants the seed for Step 2. Instead of introducing new info at the time zone check, Step 2 becomes a quick confirmation of something they already heard. Smoother flow, fewer surprises.
Step 2 Time Zone Verification
Issues identified
Asking “what time zone are you in?” puts the burden on the client. Many people hesitate or get it wrong (Mountain vs. Central, etc.).
Nobody asks “what time zone are you in?” in normal conversation — it feels like a quiz.
The current altScript already uses a better approach (verify by checking the current time). The primary script should lead with that pattern instead.
Current
Primary script
“And just to confirm — what time zone are you in? I know we texted earlier but I want to make sure we have the right time for you.”
Alt script
“So it should be 1:45 PM for you right now, right? Okay awesome, perfect. So your appointment is {Day} at {Time} your time.”
Suggested
Primary script
“I just want to make sure we have your time right — it should be about {CurrentTimeInTheirZone} for you right now, right?”
Alt → “After they confirm”
“Perfect, so your appointment is {Day} at {Time} your time.”
Why
Verify by checking, not quizzing. Fewer words, feels like a casual check instead of a test, and gets you to the same answer faster. The caller already glances at the area code during ringing — use that info instead of asking the client to self-report.
Also update
Instruction text: Change to “ALWAYS verify on the phone, even if they texted it. AI Blaze/Aloware can show 3 different times. Verify BY CHECKING, not quizzing — glance at their area code to guess their zone, then confirm.”
Tip: Add “Don’t ask them to name their time zone — many people hesitate or get it wrong.”
Step 3 Zoom Access & Calendar Invite
Issues identified
Two questions stacked together. The first (“Were you able to find...”) is a yes/no that lets them say “yeah I think so” without actually checking.
The instruction says “Make them GO FIND IT” — but the script uses soft questions instead of directives.
Alt script (“Could you try to look for it...”) repeats the same request when the first ask already failed. Doesn’t solve the problem.
Current
Primary script
“Were you able to find that calendar invitation in your email? Can you see the Zoom link in there?”
If “I don’t know / I get a bunch of emails”
“No worries! Could you try to look for it right now and see that calendar invitation with the Zoom link?”
Suggested
Primary script
“Can you pull up your email real quick and find the calendar invite? I’ll wait — I want to make sure the Zoom link is in there.”
If they can’t find it
“No worries — let me resend it to you right now. Check your email in about 30 seconds and let me know when you see it.”
Why
“Pull up your email real quick” is a directive, not a question. “I’ll wait” signals you’re not moving on until they actually check. The alt now takes action (resend) instead of re-asking the same question. Keeps momentum instead of stalling.
Also update
Instruction text: Add “Give a directive, not a question.”
Tip: Change to “‘I’ll wait’ signals you’re not moving on. If they still can’t find it after resend, walk them through checking spam/promotions folders.”
Step 4 Zoom Experience Check
Issues identified
Two back-to-back questions. “Virtual telehealth appointment” is jargon-heavy for many clients (often older, health-challenged).
The second question (“Have you done a Zoom call?”) is the one that actually matters — the first is redundant.
Current
“Have you done a virtual telehealth appointment before? Have you done a Zoom call before?”
Suggested
“Have you used Zoom before, or will this be your first time?”
Why
One question instead of two. Plain language instead of jargon. “Or will this be your first time?” normalizes saying no — the client doesn’t feel dumb admitting it. Gets to the same branching decision in half the words.
Also update
Instruction text: Add “One simple question.”
Tip: Lead with “‘Or will this be your first time?’ normalizes saying no — they don’t feel dumb admitting it.”
Step 5 Device Push
Issues identified
Two stacked questions again. “Computer” and “laptop” are basically the same thing to most people — listing both sounds like padding.
Alt script bundles two separate ideas (device push + pen/paper) into one long paragraph. By the time you get to “pen and paper,” they’ve tuned out.
Current
Primary script
“What device are you going to use for this call? Do you have a computer, a laptop, an iPad?”
If “just my phone”
“You could do it on your phone, but do you have a laptop or an iPad? We really recommend a bigger screen so you can see the charts {HAName} will be going over. It makes a big difference. Also, have a pen and paper handy — this is like a real doctor’s appointment, but virtually over Zoom.”
Suggested
Primary script
“What are you planning to do the Zoom on — laptop, iPad, or phone?”
If “just my phone”
“Totally fine, but if you have a laptop or iPad available, I’d really use that — {HAName} shares some charts on screen and they’re hard to read on a small phone. Worth it if you can swing it.”
Then add separately (after they respond)
“Oh and grab a pen and paper — this is like a real doctor’s appointment, just over Zoom.”
Why
Primary: One question, conversational. Listing phone last subtly anchors laptop/iPad as the “normal” choice. Alt: Split into two separate beats so each idea gets its own moment. The device push doesn’t get diluted by pen/paper. Shorter sentences = easier to deliver naturally on a live call.
Also update
Instruction text: Add “List phone LAST to anchor bigger screens as the default.”

Patterns Across All 5 Steps

Pattern Found Fix Applied
Double-stacked questions One question at a time — easier to deliver, easier to process
Asking client to self-report (time zone, Zoom exp) Verify for them or normalize the “no” answer
Long alt scripts bundling 2+ ideas Split into sequential beats — each idea gets its own moment
Soft directives (“could you try to...”) Direct actions (“pull up your email,” “let me resend it”)
Redundant word lists (“computer, laptop, iPad”) Tighten to distinct choices — remove synonyms
